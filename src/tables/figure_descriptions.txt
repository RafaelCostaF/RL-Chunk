Figure F1 — Domain Distribution: Counts of samples per domain (topic). Purpose: quantify topical coverage and detect imbalance that may bias retrieval and evaluation. Interpretation: highly skewed bars indicate over-representation that can inflate performance in those domains.
Figure F2 — Question-Type Distribution: Counts of samples per question_type. Purpose: assess task variety (e.g., factual vs. explanatory). Interpretation: limited variety can restrict generalization of RAG behaviors.
Figure F3 — Query Token Length: Number of tokens (alphabetic + numeric) per user query. Purpose: approximate information need complexity. Interpretation: longer right tail suggests presence of multi-faceted or under-specified questions.
Figure F4 — Query Word Length (Alphabetic Only): Counts alphabetic words only, removing numeric tokens. Purpose: isolate linguistic verbosity. Interpretation: compare with F3 to see whether length is driven by numbers or by natural language.
Figure F5 — Query Length by Domain (Boxplot): Distribution of query length across domains. Purpose: identify domains where users formulate longer/more varied questions. Interpretation: larger IQRs indicate higher intra-domain heterogeneity; outliers may reflect edge cases.
Figure F6 — Query vs. Base-Text Length (Scatter): Relationship between query length and retrieved context length. Purpose: test proportionality in retrieval (do longer questions pull longer contexts?). Interpretation: positive slope suggests context length scaling with query complexity.
Figure F7 — Base-Text Token Length: Token counts of retrieved passages (page_results_text). Purpose: measure information load provided to the generator. Interpretation: very long contexts may be redundant/noisy; very short ones may be insufficient.
Figure F8 — Paragraph Count in Base Text: Number of paragraphs per retrieved passage. Purpose: structural density/segmentation proxy. Interpretation: many paragraphs often indicate heterogeneous sources or multi-section documents.
Figure F9 — Base-Text Length by Domain (Boxplot): Distribution of context length per domain. Purpose: diagnose domains demanding more/less context. Interpretation: systematic differences can imply domain-specific retrieval strategies.
Figure F10 — Numeric Content Ratio in Queries: (# tokens with digits) / (total tokens). Purpose: quantify data-centric queries (years, amounts, codes). Interpretation: high ratios suggest need for numerical grounding and precise retrieval.
Figure F11 — Numeric Content Ratio in Base Texts: same ratio applied to retrieved passages. Purpose: detect data-rich contexts (tables, statistics). Interpretation: alignment between F10 and F11 indicates retrieval capturing the expected quantitative evidence.
Figure F12 — Lexical Diversity (TTR) in Queries: Type–Token Ratio = unique words / total words. Purpose: assess vocabulary variety and redundancy. Interpretation: higher TTR implies richer vocabulary; low TTR may indicate formulaic phrasing.
Figure F13 — Lexical Diversity (TTR) in Base Texts: TTR of retrieved passages. Purpose: evaluate breadth of vocabulary in the evidence. Interpretation: high TTR may cover multiple subtopics; low TTR can mean focused, specialized language.
Figure F14 — Character Entropy in Queries: Shannon entropy over characters. Purpose: proxy for textual complexity/variability (including punctuation/numerals). Interpretation: high entropy may reflect noisier inputs or mixed formats; low entropy suggests uniform phrasing.
Figure F15 — Character Entropy in Base Texts: entropy over characters in retrieved passages. Purpose: gauge information density and stylistic variety. Interpretation: extreme values may indicate templated data (low) or heterogeneous sources (high).
Figure F16 — Lexical Overlap (Jaccard) Query vs. Base: |Vq ∩ Vb| / |Vq ∪ Vb| using token sets. Purpose: measure contextual alignment between query and retrieved evidence. Interpretation: very low overlap suggests poor retrieval; extremely high overlap may indicate redundancy rather than reasoning.
Figure F17 — Answer Token Length: token count of generated answers. Purpose: characterize response granularity. Interpretation: short answers correlate with extractive behavior; long answers suggest synthesis or explanation.
